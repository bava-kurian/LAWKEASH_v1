📌 Phase 1 – Planning & Design
1️⃣ Define Scope of Laws to Include

Clearly decide which Indian laws the system will cover in the first version to avoid scope creep.

Initial Law Coverage (Phase-1)

Indian Penal Code (IPC) – crimes, offenses, punishments

Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC) – arrest, FIR, investigation, trial process

Code of Civil Procedure (CPC) – civil cases, procedures

Indian Evidence Act – admissibility and relevance of evidence

Scope Decisions

Focus only on central (Union) laws

Exclude:

State-specific amendments

Case-law interpretations (for now)

Recent ordinances (can be added later)

✔️ Goal: Ensure accuracy over completeness in the first version.

2️⃣ Decide Response Boundaries (Information vs Advice)

This is a critical legal and ethical design decision.

What the System CAN Do

Explain:

Legal sections in simple language

Legal procedures (e.g., how FIR works)

Rights and obligations under law

Provide:

Section numbers

Act names

General legal definitions

What the System MUST NOT Do

Give legal advice (e.g., “You should file a case”)

Predict court outcomes

Suggest punishments or strategies

Replace a lawyer’s opinion

Boundary Enforcement Strategy

Use strict prompt instructions

Add mandatory disclaimer in every response:

“This information is for educational purposes only and does not constitute legal advice.”

✔️ Goal: Keep the system informational, not advisory.

3️⃣ Choose LLM Approach (Fine-Tuning vs Prompt-Based)

Decide how the language model will be adapted for Indian legal knowledge.

Option A: Prompt-Based Only

Use a general LLM

Inject law text via prompt

Faster to build

❌ Higher hallucination risk

Option B: Fine-Tuned LLM (Recommended)

Fine-tune on:

Indian legal Q&A

Section explanations

Benefits:

Better legal language understanding

Consistent tone

Reduced hallucinations

Use lightweight fine-tuning (LoRA)

Final Decision (Phase-1)

✔️ Hybrid Approach

Fine-tuned LLM

Combined with retrieval (RAG) for grounding

✔️ Goal: Balance accuracy, compute limits, and reliability.

4️⃣ Choose Retrieval Method (RAG)

To ensure answers are factually grounded, retrieval is mandatory.

Why RAG is Needed

LLMs may:

Fabricate section numbers

Mix up acts

Legal systems require source-backed answers

Retrieval Design

Store:

Bare Act sections

Cleaned legal text chunks

Convert text into:

Vector embeddings

On user query:

Retrieve relevant law sections

Inject them into the model context

Generate answer only from retrieved text

Tools (Design-Level)

Vector database: FAISS / similar

Chunk size: 300–500 tokens

Top-k retrieval: 3–5 sections

✔️ Goal: Zero hallucination, source-grounded responses.

✅ Phase-1 Outcome

By the end of Planning & Design, you will have:

Clearly defined legal scope

Strict response boundaries

Chosen LLM strategy

Decided retrieval architecture

This ensures smooth execution in Phase-2 (Data + Model setup).
